What cronoaudit does, and for whom

cronoaudit reads a schedule, checks whether it holds together, measures what is done against what was planned, projects what comes next, and writes a report in which every figure says where it came from and how to reproduce it. It looks for one specific kind of failure: the arithmetic is right, every term is right in its own source, the result is plausible, nothing errors, and the number means nothing.

For whom

For whoever has a schedule in front of them and has to say whether it holds: the schedule received from another party, the one produced in-house, or the one under their responsibility to update. The reader may sit in a contractor, a supervising body, a regulator, a consultancy, or be studying. The tool does not distinguish the role of whoever uses it; it distinguishes the file. The planner who has to deliver a schedule that passes, the engineer auditing what was received and the manager who needs a cycle report for the board use the same report.

The manual assumes no prior knowledge of the concepts. Chapter 2 explains each one from scratch. Readers who already have them can go straight to chapter 3.

What goes in and what comes out

Input
The MS Project schedule. On any system, the XML that Project itself exports, which is its documented format. On Windows with Project installed, also the .mpp itself, or the schedule open in Project. One file for the critical review; two files, previous and current, for the cycle comparison.
Output
A self-contained HTML report with no external reference, which opens in any browser and prints to PDF. Beside it, JSON files with the same data, for whoever wants it outside the report.
Language
The schedule's. The tool detects it from the content and writes the report in Portuguese or English. It can be forced.
Paths
Three: talking to an AI agent, clicking the Cronoaudit tab inside Project, or running the engine alone, with no AI. Chapter 4 shows all three.
Requirements
Python 3.9 or later; on Windows, the installer brings one if it is missing. To read the .mpp directly, MS Project on Windows. The engine does not use the network. What an agent reads goes to its provider, and chapter 6 deals with that.

What it does not do

It does not read Primavera P6: the method transfers, but nothing was measured in that format. It does not produce a contractual delay claim: the forensics reconstruct the mechanism, and a claim needs contemporaneous records and a formal method. It does not certify against the DCMA standard: the fourteen metrics are implemented and labelled as an implementation. And it never invents a distribution, a default value or a figure the file does not carry. Chapter 15 covers this in detail.

Concepts this manual assumes

Every concept below appears in the report. If one is new, read its box before going on. If all are familiar, this chapter is a vocabulary reference.

The schedule as a network

A schedule is not a list of dates. It is a network: each activity has predecessors, which must happen before it, and successors, which depend on it. The link type says how: finish-to-start is the common one, the successor only starts when the predecessor finishes; start-to-start, finish-to-finish and start-to-finish are the other three. A link can carry a lag, a mandatory wait, or a lead, a permitted overlap.

When the scheduling tool recalculates dates, it walks this network. If the network says one thing and the works do another, every calculated date rests on logic that is not being followed. That is why the report checks the network before any date.

Baseline

The baseline is a copy of the plan saved at a certain moment: dates, durations and costs as they were when the plan was approved. Everything the report calls variance, delay or pull-forward is measured against it. MS Project keeps up to eleven baselines: Baseline and Baseline 1 to 10. A schedule that has grown may hold the one in force in one that is not the default. Which one counts is a choice, and cronoaudit makes it from the data, not from a field that declares and is not confirmed.

Status date

The date up to which progress is valid: the schedule's "today". Every comparison of planned against actual is made at it. A file without a status date cannot be analysed honestly, and cronoaudit refuses to assume today's date instead, because that would change the answer every day the analysis was repeated.

Calendar and working day

Each activity has a calendar, which says which days and hours are working time. That is where holidays, the shift, the five- or six-day week and the weather reserve live, the days the works do not work because of rain. Two activities in the same schedule can have different calendars. Every duration, variance and window in the report is counted on the activity's own calendar, because counting in calendar days, or on the project calendar, gives wrong numbers in silence.

Physical percent and percent complete

They are two different fields. Percent complete is duration: how much of the activity's time has passed, and Project computes it on its own from actual dates. Physical percent is declared by a person and measures how much of the work is out. One says how much time passed; the other, how much was delivered. Earned value uses the physical one. Swapping one for the other changes the result with nothing changed on site.

Earned value and the S-curve

Earned value is each activity's baseline cost multiplied by its physical percent. Summed, it is how much of the budget has been "earned". Planned value is how much should have been earned by the status date, according to the time-phased baseline. The S-curve is the cumulative chart of both over time: planned and earned, and the gap between the curves is the delay in money. The schedule performance index, SPI, is earned divided by planned.

Float and critical path

An activity's total float is how much it can slip without moving the project finish. Zero float means any delay there pushes the end: the activity is critical. The critical path is the chain of critical activities from start to finish. An activity with float can slip until it consumes it; after that, it enters the critical path. Project computes float, early and late dates and the critical flag; cronoaudit reads those fields rather than recomputing them.

Earned Schedule

Classic earned value measures schedule in money, and that has a defect: at the end of the project earned always reaches the budget, so the schedule indicator goes to 1.0 even on a project a year late. Earned Schedule measures schedule in time: it asks on what date the planned curve reached the value earned today. That date, compared with the time elapsed, gives a schedule efficiency and an independent finish, which is set against the finish the schedule projects. Chapter 11 details the formulas.

Productivity by resource

A material resource, such as cubic metres of cut, has planned, executed and remaining quantities per activity. Productivity is quantity per working day. Comparing the practised rate with the required one says whether the activity finishes when the schedule says. cronoaudit reads the quantities from the file's own assignments, with no custom field.

The reference question

An indicator that compares planned with actual has two halves. If they are measured against different references, another baseline, another instant, another population, another unit, the difference between them means nothing, and the number comes out plausible. It is the failure cronoaudit looks for everywhere. The question that finds it: are all the terms of this calculation measured against the same reference?

Installation and first run

cronoaudit is an Agent Skill: a folder with instructions for an AI agent and the programs that do the work. It installs by copying that folder to where agents look for skills. One command does that for every agent at once, and leaves the engine ready to run without an agent too.

Install with one command

On Windows, in PowerShell: press the Windows key, type PowerShell and click Windows PowerShell. Use that window, not Command Prompt.

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Holiv/cronoaudit/main/install.ps1 | iex

On macOS or Linux, in Terminal:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Holiv/cronoaudit/main/install.sh | bash

The installer downloads the repository from GitHub, copies the skill to the two folders below, copies the fictitious example to the cronoaudit-exemplo folder and runs the tool's internal test as proof of installation. Then it asks only what is yours to choose. On Windows, if it finds MS Project, it asks whether you also want the Cronoaudit tab, which chapter 5 describes. And it asks whether you want to see the example now: it compares two revisions of an invented works and opens the report in the browser. Its messages are in Portuguese on a Portuguese system, and in English elsewhere.

At the end you see cronoaudit 1.0.0 is installed., or Pronto: cronoaudit 1.0.0 instalada. on a Portuguese system. Right below, the installer shows how to use it. With an AI agent, open the agent in the schedule's folder and ask for the review; if it was already open, close it and open it again, because skills are read at the start of each session. With no agent, the line after With no agent gives the right command for this computer. The last line runs the example again.

Python on Windowscronoaudit needs Python 3.9 or later. On Windows, if there is no working Python, the installer brings its own copy: the official package from python.org, checked against its SHA-256 before it is unpacked, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\cronoaudit\python. It needs no administrator rights and changes neither the registry nor the PATH. Deleting that folder removes this Python completely. On macOS and Linux, Python must already be installed; if it is missing, the installer stops and says so.

Reading a .mpp directly, on Windows

On Windows with MS Project installed, there is nothing to export. Pass the .mpp as it is:

python "$env:USERPROFILE\.claude\skills\cronoaudit\scripts\review.py" rodovia-ficticia-rev04.mpp

The engine asks Project itself to export a copy as XML, as a person would through File, Save As. The file is opened read-only, exported and closed without saving. It does not change. If it is already open in Project, your window is not touched: the file as saved on disk is copied to a temporary folder, and Project exports the copy. Changes not yet saved stay out of the analysis, and the engine says so. Project marks a file as changed just by opening it, so that note can appear with nothing changed.

If Project was closed, it opens visibly, so any question it asks shows on screen, and closes at the end. If it does not answer within ten minutes, the engine stops and says how to export by hand. The temporary XML is deleted when the run ends, and the reports land beside the .mpp. No setting of Project, Office or Windows is changed.

For the schedule open in Project, use --from-project. With a file given as well, that file is the previous version:

python "$env:USERPROFILE\.claude\skills\cronoaudit\scripts\review.py" --from-project
python "$env:USERPROFILE\.claude\skills\cronoaudit\scripts\review.py" rodovia-ficticia-rev03.mpp --from-project

Off Windows, or without Project, the .mpp cannot be exported there. The engine says so and asks for the XML, which whoever has Project exports through File, Save As, XML Format. It is Project's own documented format, and it carries everything the analysis needs: tasks, links with lag, the eleven baselines, calendars, custom fields, assignments and the time-phased data. Off Windows there is also an optional .mpp reader through the mpxj library, with Java installed. It is never required and it has not been verified.

Verify the tool before trusting it

python3 ~/.claude/skills/cronoaudit/scripts/test_checks.py

The test runs the tool over synthetic schedules. On the positive one, every check must fire. On the negative one, every check must stay silent. Without the second half a clean result is worth nothing: a probe that cannot fire reports zero findings on a broken file as readily as on a sound one. The installer runs this test at the end, after copying the example. The test also checks the fictitious example's fourteen problems, which it looks for in the examples/ folder of a copy of the repository or in ~/cronoaudit-exemplo. If the example is in neither, that part is skipped, and the test says so.

A. Through the agent

With the skill installed, no command needs to be remembered. The agent reads the skill's instructions, runs the engine, reads the results and talks about them. The figures always come from the engine; the agent orchestrates, interprets and writes. Use the agent you prefer, or the one your company allows on your computer.

Where to open

The agent reads the local disk, so it has to know where the file is. Open the terminal in the schedule's folder and start the agent there, or start anywhere and give the full path in the first message. The agent looks for .mpp and .xml files in that folder only, not in its subfolders. When both exist with the same name, it takes the .mpp, which is the original. If there is more than one schedule, it lists them and asks which, and offers the comparison when two look like versions of the same one. It never picks silently.

The first message

Analyse rodovia-ficticia-rev04.xml.
Compare rev03 with rev04 and tell me what changed, by topic.
Analyse the schedule open in Project.
Which milestones are at risk of missing their deadline?
Analyse rodovia-ficticia-rev04.xml and save the results in ~/Reports/october.

The language is free. If the agent does not recognise the skill, close and start the agent again and ask which skills it has. In Copilot, /skills reload reloads the skills.

What happens next

In a first conversation, before reading any file, the agent says where the data goes: what it reads goes to its provider, and the engine runs locally, sending nothing. Then it settles the mode, free or secret, as chapter 6 describes. Only then does it run the analysis, once. The analysis and the report are the same command: the HTML report and the JSON files are written beside the schedule, or in the folder named in the message. The agent states the report's full path in its first reply. After that the conversation continues over the data already written, without running again, unless you ask or the file changes.

The report does not have to be opened for the conversation to happen. A finding, a rate or a milestone can be discussed for an hour before the HTML is opened, or it may never be opened. The executive synthesis is written only when asked for. After a comparison, the agent offers in one line the synthesis and the list of what to ask the other party.

Free mode and secret mode

The engine sends nothing, in either mode. The mode is about what an agent reads. In free mode, the agent reads the schedule and the results, and what it reads goes to its provider. In secret mode, the agent reads only a coded copy, and the report with the real data is built on your computer.

Where your data goes

  • The engine runs on your computer and does not use the network.
  • The installers download the repository from GitHub and, on Windows and only if needed, Python from python.org. Nothing else.
  • The agent is different. What it reads (the schedule, the report, your questions) goes to its provider, under the terms of your account or your company's. Check the company's policy before using an agent with a contract schedule.
  • If the policy does not allow an agent, use path C, or the tab's Sem IA buttons.

The vault and the work folder

What decoding needs lives in a local vault, in the same folder as the preference: ~/.cronoaudit/vault. It holds each project's key, with the code table, the date origin and the money factor, and each run's real outputs. The vault never goes into the work folder. A project is the folder of the current schedule, or the name given with --project. rev03 and rev04 in the same folder share codes, so codes compare across versions.

The work folder is new on every run, with a name that says nothing about the schedule. From the command, it is created in ~/cronoaudit-work, or wherever --workdir says; from the tab, in %USERPROFILE%\cronoaudit-work\<date-time>\<code>. It holds only the coded copy; a cronoaudit-secret.json, which lists the files and explains the codes; the instructions for the agent, in CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md; and, for Claude Code, the guard, in .claude/. A leak test, run with the internal test, checks that the folder holds only coded files.

Running in secret mode

From the tab, answer Yes to the mode question. Without the tab, you run one command yourself, in your own terminal, with the .xml or, on Windows with Project, with the .mpp:

python "$env:USERPROFILE\.claude\skills\cronoaudit\scripts\review.py" rodovia-ficticia-rev04.mpp --secret

The command shows the work folder and says to open the agent inside it: cd to the folder, then claude, copilot or codex. The first thing the agent says is that you should refer to activities by code or by row number, because whatever you type with a real name goes to the AI service.

It has to be you who runs it. An agent that holds the original file protects nothing. So when you ask an agent for secret mode, it does not run the engine: it shows you the command to run in your own terminal. And the engine, when it sees that Claude Code started it, refuses secret mode and stops having read nothing. If that happens, run the command in a PowerShell opened from the Start menu, or open Project from the Start menu, and not from inside an agent's terminal. Another version, or a comparison, is a new coded run, started by you.

The real report, at the end

When you ask, the agent runs secret_decode.py finish. The command decodes the work folder's report on your computer, with the synthesis the agent wrote, if any. It checks that the result equals, value for value, what the engine wrote in the vault. Then it writes the real report beside the original schedule and in the vault, and opens it in the browser. From the tab, the real report appears in the run folder. On screen, the command shows counts only, never a name, a value, a date or a path. If the check fails, it writes the engine's real output instead and says how many values differed. After its first reading, the agent offers in one line the synthesis and the report with real names, and does neither unless you ask.

Reading an answer with real names

The agent's answers come in codes. To read one of them with the real names, dates and values, run in your own terminal, outside the agent, the command the agent itself gives you:

python "$env:USERPROFILE\.claude\skills\cronoaudit\scripts\secret_decode.py" text "<work folder>"

Paste the answer and end with Ctrl+Z and Enter on a line of its own, on Windows, or with Ctrl+D, on macOS and Linux. The translation appears on your screen only. That command is yours: it prints real names, so the agent never runs it. It refuses to run inside Claude Code, and the guard stops it too.

The guard, in Claude Code only

In Claude Code, the work folder carries deny rules and a hook. They let the session read only its own folder, run only the skill's finishing commands and use only the tools a secret session needs. PowerShell, connectors such as OneDrive, SharePoint and Microsoft 365, sending files and messaging other sessions are closed, and a tool not on the list starts closed. The hook is a tripwire against the agent leaving the folder by habit or by a misread instruction. It is not a sandbox against an agent set on getting out.

Claude Code asks whether you trust each new folder. Say yes: that is what turns the guard on, and without it the guard stays off. The question comes back on every analysis, because each has its own folder.

In Codex and GitHub Copilot, the folder carries the same rules in writing. Nothing technical stops the agent from opening other files.

What secret mode answers, and what it does not

It answers about logic, float, critical path, durations, progress, cost proportions and weights, differences between versions, network quality, deviations and inconsistencies, and the forensic chain by row number. It does not answer about real values, company, supplier or person names, locations, and absolute dates, such as "does this fall before the March contractual milestone?". For those questions, the agent refuses plainly and points to the way out: the decoded report on your computer, your own secret_decode.py text, or free mode.

The limits

  • The codes do not make the schedule anonymous: its structure, durations and row numbers stay readable.
  • Coding lowers what leaves the computer. It does not replace your company's policy on AI services.

The report, section by section

The report is a single HTML file. It opens in any browser, works offline, prints to PDF with one button, and switches between light and dark themes. Activity tables are collapsed, so the report does not open as a wall of rows; printing expands them.

Cover and verdict

The cover carries the file, the number of leaf activities, the status date and the baseline cost. The verdict shows cumulative physical progress weighted by cost, beside a fixed text on how to read those numbers, and the first Reading block: sentences generated by rule from this analysis's figures. When activities sit in violated logic, a red notice says to settle that before discussing any date. The notice is as wide as the breach: with up to three pairs, none on an unfinished critical activity or on the chain that holds the finish, it names the pairs and the milestones they reach, instead of condemning the whole network. With no links at all, the report says there is no network to hold.

When an executive synthesis has been injected, it appears right below the cover, with the name of whoever wrote it and the time, so prose is never mistaken for measurement.

Deadlines and milestones

Right after the verdict and the cards comes section PZ, which answers the question a delivery is read for. Each future milestone appears against its deadline, in calendar days: the margin in the current schedule, the margin after the slippage that 80% of activities did not exceed, whether the milestone is critical, and the row where its delay starts on the driving path. A negative margin is past the deadline.

On the fictitious example

On rev04, the seven future milestones finish within their deadline. The smallest margin is row 9's, Conclusão do Viaduto V-01: one day. With the 80% slippage, seven days, three more milestones pass their date, rows 7, 8 and 9. Row 9's delay starts at row 148, bored piles, with eleven days against the baseline.

The findings

Each finding is a block with code, severity, title and plain-language subtitle. Inside, three fixed blocks: problem, impact and solution. Then the criterion with the counting convention, where it came from, with the Project columns, and how to reproduce it in the tool. Finally the activity table, collapsed. Only the visible row number identifies an activity, because it is the only identifier a person can find on screen. In the network findings the table shows the explicit pair, successor and predecessor, with the link type and the reason. Findings that came out at zero are gathered in a single line, checked with nothing found, so the report does not open with empty blocks.

Conventions

The last section lists what the report assumed: the baseline used and why it was chosen, the threshold and its unit, the tolerance, the population, the counting convention, how working days were counted, where the percentage comes from, the detected language and how confident the detection was. Nobody has to guess anything.

The reading layer

Every section opens with sentences generated by rule from its own figures. They say what the data supports and nothing more; if a figure is missing, the sentence does not appear. It is what lets the report be handed to someone who was not in the room. The executive synthesis is something else: a written text, injected with author and time, which chapter 13 explains.

The nine checks

Each check has a stable code, which stays the same when the implementation changes. Without that a problem cannot be discussed across weeks. The order matters: network first, then dates, then reporting.

Population and cross-cutting rules

Every check looks only at leaf activities, active and not external. Summaries stay out, because they would count their children twice. Milestones are in everything except G.

Pending reporting migrates firstA predecessor at 100% physical with no actual finish is a reporting defect, finding P. Its links are not evaluated for A1 or A2. Without this rule, a reporting problem would be reported as an execution problem, and the conversation with whoever executes would start by accusing the wrong thing.

A1 · Out-of-sequence execution

A successor started while the predecessor had not finished, or finished after the successor's start beyond what the link allows.

Problem
The network describes an order that execution does not practise.
Impact
Every forecast date is computed on a false premise. And the delay of stalled predecessors stops propagating to milestones, because the successors have already been delivered: the plan looks healthy at the end because it is broken in the middle.
Solution
Settle before discussing any date. Either the link is wrong, or the sequence was broken on site and the consequence enters the forecast. Do not delete the link to silence the warning: it is the only record that the order was not followed.

The criterion evaluates the four link types, with actual dates, on the successor's calendar, honouring lag. An overlap covered by a lead is not flagged. Pairs with both activities complete leave the count, by decision, and are recorded in the data with the overlap in days, because the history of out-of-order execution is evidence for the forensics.

To reproduce in Project

Group by predecessor and filter by Actual Start populated. Mark the pair and inspect the link in the Gantt.

A2 · Total inversion

A successor is complete and the predecessor never started. It is the extreme form of a broken sequence, and it is unanswerable in a meeting. Either the sequence was invented for the plan, or the progress landed on the wrong activity. Check pair by pair and record which, because the answer varies between pairs.

To reproduce

Filter by Actual Finish populated and check the Actual Start of each predecessor.

H · Forecast elapsed with no actual

A forecast start is in the past with no actual start, or a forecast finish is in the past with no actual finish. The plan says it happened; nothing in the file says it happened. Every successor is projected from a date that already failed to hold. Two possibilities with different answers: the work happened and was not recorded, or it did not happen and the forecast must move. Leaving it is the only wrong option.

E · Pulled forward and never started

The forecast finish moved earlier than the baseline by 30 working days or more, on the activity's calendar, and the activity never started. Progress pulled earlier with no execution behind it is a plan change dressed as performance, and in aggregate it can offset real delay elsewhere. Ask what changed to justify the date. If nothing did, the forecast is optimistic and should go back.

C · Delayed beyond the threshold

The forecast finish moved later than the baseline by 30 working days or more, on the activity's calendar. Completed activities stay in the list on purpose: consummated delay is information. It is the finding the other party expects and the easiest to contest, which is why it needs the reproduction attached. Read it with the weight distribution: the same number of delayed activities means different things depending on the budget share they carry. The float column says how much margin is left.

Threshold in working daysThe 30 days are counted on the activity's own calendar, not in calendar days nor on the project shift. On a six-day week, 30 working days are about 35 calendar days. The unit is declared in the conventions section and adjustable through the profile.

G · Duration inconsistent with the window

The duration does not match the working time between start and finish, measured on the activity's own calendar, with partial first and last days. G is a thermometer, not a finding: it says something was edited inconsistently, not what. Start anchored on the actual date with the remainder pushed, a typed date over a computed one, a constraint fighting the logic. Use it to choose what to inspect, looking at the largest differences first. Never report it alone.

B · Milestone with no deadline

The milestone has no Deadline set, so nothing in the file makes its date binding. Without it the milestone shows comfortable float even with the contractual date passed, because the schedule was never told the date mattered. A deadline is not a constraint: it moves nothing, it only reveals slippage. Register contractual dates on the milestones that have them; it changes no calculation and makes every following report tell the truth about float. B is optional by default, because not every organisation practises it; the profile promotes it to a finding.

F · In progress at zero percent

Actual start, no actual finish, and duration percent complete at zero. On a schedule reported by physical percent this fires a lot, because the duration percent is not maintained; that is why it is treated as a check. On a schedule reported by duration, it is the real signal of a stalled activity.

P · Pending reporting

One hundred percent physical with no actual finish date. It is a reporting defect, and the distinction decides who the conversation is with. Kept among the network findings, it would accuse the team of executing out of sequence when the work was done and only the date is missing. Enter the actual finish. Until then the activity stays deliberately out of the network findings.

How to read it

Read Q1, Q2, Q5 and Q7 before any date. A schedule that fails them is not a model of the works, and every date it produces is a number with no basis. Q6 and Q8 say whether it can be controlled. Q11 and Q14 say whether it was met. Beyond the fourteen, the report shows summaries with links, which should not exist, milestones with no deadline, the number of incomplete critical activities, and whether the critical chain reaches the final milestone, which is the test of the network being whole.

On the fictitious example

On rev04, rows 77, 89, 136 and 170 have no successor and appear in Q1: 4 of 96, below the threshold, so the network is read as linked. The same four, plus row 57, carry float above 44 working days and appear in Q6. Of 43 activities due by the status date, 28 finished on time: the baseline execution index is 0.65, below the 0.95 floor, and Q11 fails too. The final milestone has a deadline, so Q13 is computed: 1.025.

Productivity and trend by resource

Section V6 answers, for every activity in progress with a quantity, when it finishes at the actual rate, and what that does to the baseline and the critical path. Everything comes from the file's own assignments; no custom field is required.

Where the quantities live

For a material resource, Project stores the quantity in the work fields, as hours of a duration. An assignment with 10,567 hours of work on a resource labelled m³ is 10,567 m³. The same rule gives executed, remaining, baseline planned and executed per day. An assignment with no resource cannot be trended, and the report counts how many there are.

Three rates, not one

One rate alone is not a fair reading: a cut activity hit by a fortnight of rain shows a rate that says nothing about the crew.

RateHowWhat it answers
ownexecuted on this activity divided by working days elapsed since its actual start, on its calendarwhat is happening here, rain included
globaleverything executed by the resource on all fronts divided by the working days those assignments tookthe fair scenario, which dilutes a bad fortnight

The own rate divides by elapsed days, including days when nothing came out. A crew that produced on eight of ten days practised the ten-day rate. The planned rate also comes out, baseline quantity over baseline duration, and two required rates: remaining over working days to the forecast finish, and to the late finish.

Four dates, three verdicts

The remainder runs over each rate, in working days of the activity's calendar from the status date, and lands against four dates: forecast finish, baseline finish, late finish, which the file already carries with the float built in, and the projected finish.

One case read end to end

Baseline on 30 September, forecast on the 26th, twenty days of float. If the rate projects 28 September, it is reprogram: the forecast slips, the baseline does not. If it projects 2 October, the baseline slips two days and eighteen days of float remain: no impact on the end date. If it projects more than twenty days beyond the forecast, it enters the critical path, and the date that happens appears in the table.

Thin evidence

A rate built on a few days, or on a tiny share of the planned quantity, projects absurdity with complete ease: a resource at two percent executed "finishes" in 2031. The rate is reported, because it is what the data says, but it is flagged as thin evidence when it has under ten days of data or under two percent of the planned quantity, and the verdict carries the flag. Executed derived from physical percent, rather than posted quantity, is also flagged as inferred. Read those as a question, not a verdict. The thresholds are adjustable through the profile.

Contracted productivity

If the organisation keeps a contracted productivity per crew in a custom field, the profile can point to it and it appears as a comparison column beside the rates. It is an optional layer, never a requirement.
